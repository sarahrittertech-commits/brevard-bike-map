# Personas

Three people this app is built for. They are deliberately few — a two-week
build cannot serve a broad audience well.

## 1. The local parent

**Wants:** to get the kids across town to a playground or the pool without
putting them on a road with logging trucks.

**Behaviour:** rides a few times a month in good weather, always with company,
always with a destination in mind. Will not ride a route she hasn't confirmed
is safe.

**What she needs from the app:** to see that a continuous bike-friendly
connection exists before leaving the house. Distance matters less than whether
the whole route is rideable with a child.

**Design implication:** route confidence is the feature. A route shown must be
one the app is actually confident about, which argues for curated routes over
generated ones.

## 2. The visiting cyclist

**Wants:** to know what's within riding distance of where he's staying, and
what's worth riding to.

**Behaviour:** in town for a weekend, likely for Pisgah. Has a bike. Doesn't
know the town. Will use the app once or twice and never again.

**What he needs from the app:** immediate orientation with no setup. Show him
the map and the destinations without asking him to sign up or configure
anything.

**Design implication:** zero onboarding. First launch shows the map with
everything on it. This is a large part of why accounts are out of scope.

## 3. The after-work rider

**Wants:** a short ride with a purpose — ride to the brewery, ride for ice
cream, string two or three stops together.

**Behaviour:** rides most weeks, knows the town well, is looking for variety
rather than information.

**What she needs from the app:** ideas. This is the persona the "adventures"
feature (R8) serves — curated multi-stop rides she wouldn't have assembled
herself.

**Design implication:** justifies R8 if time allows, and nothing else. This
persona is already served adequately by her own knowledge, so she is the
lowest-priority of the three.

---

## Who this app is not for

- Serious road or gravel cyclists planning long routes. Strava and Komoot
  serve them well already.
- Anyone outside Brevard.
- People looking for trail conditions, difficulty ratings, or mountain bike
  trail beta. That is Pisgah's domain and a different app.
